Hi, I’m Jihye Kim. I also go by Jessica. I received my Ph.D. in Information Systems from KAIST in 2025. I am currently completing an M.S. in Natural Language Processing in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at UC Santa Cruz. Before my Ph.D., I spent three years at Deloitte Consulting in Seoul, working as a management consultant on digital transformation, analytics, and organizational strategy.

My research asks how the design of digital technologies shapes the information people receive and the decisions they make. My early work focused on firms’ adoption of new IT services and on digital platforms. I continue to pursue these topics while extending this research to AI systems that generate advice and act on users’ behalf, with a particular focus on evaluating their judgments and behavior.

Research Themes

How do digital technologies shape behavior and interactions, and what are the consequences for individuals, organizations, and markets?

Economics of IS & Digital Platforms

My work on firms’ adoption of new IT services and digital platforms examines how these technologies affect consumers, businesses, and markets. Using causal inference, I study earned wage access, platform governance, and self-service technologies. I am particularly interested in how their effects vary across stakeholders with different resources, constraints, and market positions, and what explains these differences.

Economics of Information Systems · Platform Governance

Human–AI Interaction & Trustworthy AI

I develop evaluation frameworks to diagnose failure modes in language models and AI system architectures to address them. My work examines whether models distinguish helpful guidance from misleading persuasion, accurately represent user preferences, and respect information boundaries in multi-agent systems. I use these evaluations to guide architectural changes and test whether they improve reliability in interactions with users and other agents.

Multi-Agent Systems · AI Evaluation · Trustworthy AI
