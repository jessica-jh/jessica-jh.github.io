I'm a Ph.D. from KAIST (Information Systems), and I'm finishing an additional M.S. in NLP in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at UC Santa Cruz. My doctoral training was in causal inference and econometrics. I've since moved into AI evaluation and agentic AI safety and security, studying where LLM agents quietly stop behaving the way they should and how to build benchmarks that catch it.

My first paper as lead author, on earned-wage access and its effect on saving behavior, was published in Information Systems Research (UTD24/FT50). At UCSC, I've been working on negotiation agents that learn to deceive, methods for catching agents that covertly encode data, and safety benchmarks for mental-health apps and enterprise multi-agent systems.

Before graduate school, I spent three years as a Senior Consultant at Deloitte Consulting in Seoul, turning vague executive requests into KPI systems and dashboards people actually used.

AI Safety & Evaluation
Coercion Suppression Increases Preference Hallucinations via a Deceptive Bypass in K-Level Negotiation Agents Accepted
TrustNLP at ACL 2026
Jihye Kim* (Single Author)
Built an Observer–Planner–Actor negotiation agent with an appraisal gate to test what happens when you filter out obvious coercion. Filters cut overt coercion from 35% to 6%, but the same reasoning resurfaces as preference hallucination near baseline levels (33–37%). Net deception barely moves. Surface filtering alone isn't enough.
GitHub Paper
MIRAGE: A Polarity-Flipping Encoding Subspace in LLM Agents Accepted
ICML 2026 Workshop on Agentic AI: Wild Problems and Rigorous Solutions (AIWILD)
Pratibha Revankar, Kargi Chauhan, Jihye Kim, Sadiba Nusrat Nur, Vincent Siu, Chenguang Wang
Found a shared subspace in LLM residual streams that lights up when an agent covertly encodes data (Base64, ROT13, acrostics, and others). A probe trained on eight encoding families generalizes to a held-out ninth at AUC 0.975–1.000, and a polarity flip at the planning token flags inline execution versus tool delegation before any encoded text exists. Real-time monitoring hits AUC 0.918, versus 0.518 for output-only detection.
Paper
SlugRAG: Domain-Specific Multi-Turn Retrieval Accepted
SemEval at ACL 2026
Pratibha Revankar, Jihye Kim, Umit Azirakhmet
A hybrid dense-sparse retrieval pipeline (BGE-M3 plus BM25) with conversational query rewriting for a shared task on multi-turn RAG. Beat zero-shot baselines by 44.3% on Recall@10 across four QA domains.
Paper
Right or Wrong, Models Comply: Directional Blindness in LLM Moral Judgment Preprint
Jihye Kim*, Jeffrey Flanigan
A controlled evaluation of how authority and majority cues shift model judgment while task, format, and answer space stay fixed. Scaled to roughly 972K responses across nine models, three prompting conditions, and two domains, with metrics that separate beneficial compliance from a model just going along with the crowd.
arXiv
Optimizing Verbal Calibration: Structural Prompting for Reducing Confidence Error in LLMs Under Review
EMNLP 2026
Jihye Kim*, Umit Azirakhmet, Sophia Yang
Prompting interventions that narrow the gap between how confident a model sounds and how often it's right, tested on medical and legal reasoning tasks across models from 4B to 80B parameters. A skeptical self-auditing intervention improved calibration in every pairing tried, without costing accuracy.
GitHub
Evaluation Framework for Enterprise Multi-Agent Security In Progress
UC Santa Cruz · with Peng Qi, Ph.D. (Uniphore)
Peng Qi, Jihye Kim, et al.
A benchmark for multi-agent LLM systems that leak information across role-based access boundaries. Building the attack taxonomy, synthetic dialogue data, and success/leakage metrics from scratch, with a pilot already running across one-shot, multi-turn, framing, and collusive attacks.
Evaluating and Fine-Tuning LLMs for Long-Term Supportive Value in Mental-Health Conversations In Progress
UC Santa Cruz · with Prof. Magy Seif El-Nasr · in collaboration with Stanford University
Shukla, Jihye Kim, et al.
Building an evaluation framework that measures whether LLM responses in emotional-support conversations actually help someone over the long run, versus quietly encouraging them to keep relying on the model instead of people in their life. We're simulating multi-turn conversations across different user styles, scoring them with an LLM-judge pipeline and inter-rater checks, and fine-tuning models to reduce that pattern.
Causal Inference & Econometrics (IS)
Working Daily, Paid Monthly? Effects of On-Demand Earned Wage Access on the Financial Well-Being of Low-Wage Workers Published
Information Systems Research (ISR) · UTD24/FT50 · 2026
Jihye Kim*, Seokchae Yoon, Sunghun Chung, Wonseok Oh
Four thousand low-wage workers, 18 months of data, four identification strategies (staggered DID, PSM, IV, double ML). On-demand wage access raises financial-monitoring time by 12.9% and saving frequency by 3.7%, validated with a randomized experiment on Prolific (n = 312).
Journal Media
Beyond Efficiency: The Impact of Self-Order Kiosk Adoption on Demand Variety Under Review
Manufacturing & Service Operations Management (MSOM) · UTD24/FT50
Jihye Kim*, Seokchae Yoon, Anindya Ghose, Wonseok Oh
Self-order kiosks don't just speed up checkout, they change what people order. Staggered DID and IPTW on 17 months of data from 2,000+ stores show a 13.6% jump in order customization, landing unevenly across income levels.
The Economics of In-App Payment Options: Implications for Digital Platform Governance Under Review
Journal of Management Information Systems (JMIS) · FT50
Jihye Kim*, Hyeokkoo Eric Kwon, Wonseok Oh, Kunshin Im
When Google mandated in-app payment fees, spending didn't just drop, it reallocated. App-week DID and triple-differences on a 35,000-user panel show the shift varied by income tier and app ranking.
