Research Themes

Reshaping the Decision Environment

Some technologies shape decisions without making them: they change when people can act, which options stand out, and how choices are made. I study these shifts in fintech, platform governance, and service automation, using causal inference on large-scale behavioral data. A recurring pattern in my work is that the gains and burdens of the same technology fall unevenly across users, firms, and their partners, and that design choices, such as fee structures and interface layouts, shape who benefits.

Economics of Information Systems · Digital Platforms · Causal Inference

AI in the Decision Loop

As AI systems give advice, negotiate, and act on users’ behalf, they take part in decisions rather than only informing them. I build diagnostics for how these systems fail in such roles. Across nine language models, I find that models separate helpful from misleading pushback on factual questions but follow both at nearly equal rates on moral ones, a failure mode I term direction-blind moral compliance. In negotiation agents, I show that filtering coercive outputs opens a deceptive bypass: agents stop coercing but begin misrepresenting their own priorities. I am extending this work to study how people come to rely on AI systems and what that reliance means for organizations.

AI Evaluation · Multi-Agent Systems · Human–AI Decision-Making
